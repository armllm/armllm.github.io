**Subject:** Hack Armenia — 76 engineers, 24 hours, 16 AI tools built for Armenian public services

Hi [Name],

Thanks for your interest — here's the short version, with a fuller pack attached.

**What happened.** In Yerevan in August we ran two connected events. First, the **Armenia LLM
Summer School** (3–7 August): five days teaching how modern AI language models — the technology
behind ChatGPT — are built and, importantly, how to tell when they're wrong. It's taught by working
researchers from NVIDIA, Perceptron AI and IST/Unbabel, most of them Armenians who come back to
Yerevan to teach.

Then, immediately after, **Hack Armenia** (8–9 August): a *hackathon*. If the format is unfamiliar,
it's a time-boxed building competition — teams of three or four get a theme and a deliberately short
window, here 24 hours, to build and demonstrate working software. Nothing is presented that the team
didn't build in the room, and a jury scores what actually runs. The deadline is the point: it forces
people to prove an idea works instead of describing one.

**Why we do it.** Frontier AI training sits in a handful of labs abroad. The school brings that
teaching to Yerevan so people can learn it without leaving the country. The hackathon makes sure the
week ends in something built, not just understood — and this year every team had to build for one
theme: **Armenia's public interest**.

**Who took part.** 110 people applied; 76 took part in 20 teams — ML researchers, security engineers,
data scientists, students and first-time hackathon entrants, from Yerevan, Gyumri, Dilijan, Ijevan,
Hrazdan and abroad. 12 senior AI mentors worked with the teams overnight; a 10-person jury judged the
demos, including the **First Deputy Minister of High-Tech Industry**, and people from OpenAI, NVIDIA,
Nebius, YerevaNN and ServiceNow Research.

**Sponsors.** **OpenAI** sponsored the prizes with API credits — $12,000, $7,500 and $5,000 for the
top three teams, i.e. computing credit to keep developing the work. **Eleveight AI** gave a separate
special prize of $5,000-equivalent in GPUs. The event was supported by the **Ministry of High-Tech
Industry of Armenia** and **ISAA**; **Nebius** and **Yerevan State University** provided the computing
power for the school; **AI9 Startup Campus** hosted, and co-organised alongside **YerevaNN**.

**The results.** 16 working prototypes in 24 hours:

- **1st — OpenWater Armenia:** ask questions in plain language about any region's water data; it
  answers from published water, climate and infrastructure records and shows its sources.
- **2nd — COPA:** turns Armenian-language citizen comments into structured cases for public
  institutions, routes them to the right agency and flags what needs a human.
- **3rd — Armenian Socratic Tutor (Team Random):** helps fifth-graders through maths problems
  *without* giving the answer, grounded in Armenia's official textbook.
- **Special prize — DeepClare (team HI):** turns commercial invoices into draft customs declarations
  for Armenia and the EAEU, and refuses to guess when the evidence is thin.

Others tackled emergency dispatch, legal research over ARLIS legislation, lab-report explanation for
patients, crop monitoring for farmers and cybersecurity triage. **All the code is public on GitHub**,
so anything here can be checked.

One thing worth stressing: these are 24-hour prototypes, not deployed state systems — working
demonstrations of what's possible. Several teams intend to continue.
